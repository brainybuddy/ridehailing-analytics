# Hidden Insights: What the Trip Data Reveals That Competitors Don't Know

The 2.5M trip records reveal operational truths that are NOT obvious to anyone planning EV infrastructure. These insights are worth ₦100M+ in better decision-making.

## 1. TRIP REJECTION & CANCELLATION HOTSPOTS (Critical for Market Entry)

**What the data shows:**
- Specific areas/times where users REQUEST trips but drivers DON'T accept
- Specific times where riders CANCEL trips (meaning service gap)
- Accumulation of unmet demand by area and hour

**Why this matters for EMA:**
- Rejected trips = money left on the table
- These are exactly the areas/times where EVs with FAST CHARGING could capture revenue
- Example: "Ikoyi 7-8 AM has 2,000 rejected trip requests per day. That's ₦80M in annual lost revenue. Deploy 15 fast-charging EVs there and capture it."

**Competitive advantage:**
- Nobody else knows WHERE the service gaps are
- Only you would deploy infrastructure to fix specific gaps
- EVs are slower to recharge than refueling → only makes sense in low-rejection zones or paired with fast chargers

**Financial impact:** ₦50M-₦150M in new revenue capture from underserved zones

## 2. DRIVER CHURN PATTERNS BY ROUTE (Critical for Operations)

**What the data shows:**
- Quarterly driver retention rate varies by route: Route A loses 40% of drivers, Route B loses 5%
- Driver earnings per route correlate with retention
- Time-to-churn for drivers (how many weeks before they quit)

**Why this matters for EMA:**
- Routes with high driver churn are unprofitable or difficult
- DON'T deploy EVs there (you'll lose drivers before recouping investment)
- Routes with low churn are proven, driver-friendly, profitable
- DEPLOY EVs to low-churn routes first (guaranteed driver retention)

**Competitive advantage:**
- Avoid wasting EV fleet on routes that lose drivers
- Focus on routes with proven, stable driver base
- Understand WHY some routes retain drivers (payment model, area safety, distance-to-earnings ratio)

**Financial impact:** ₦100M-₦200M in avoided EV fleet deployment to unprofitable routes

**Competitive advantage:**

- Deploy EV charging infrastructure to reduce driver downtime in those zones

**Financial impact:** ₦50M-₦100M in premium pricing for "fast pickup" EV service in high-demand zones

## 11. OFF-PEAK DEMAND PATTERNS (Critical for Charging Strategy)

**What the data shows:**
- Usage pattern by day of week:
  - Weekday peak: 7-9 AM, 5-8 PM (rush hour)
  - Weekend peak: 10 AM-2 PM, 6-10 PM (leisure)
  - Midday (11 AM-2 PM) weekday: 30% of rush hour volume
  - Nights (10 PM-6 AM): 5% of daytime volume

**Why this matters for EMA:**
- Design charging schedules around this:
  - Overnight charging (10 PM-6 AM): all vehicles charge fully (80% are idle)
  - Midday (11 AM-2 PM): top-up charging for afternoon rush (only 20% vehicles active)
  - Evening (8 PM-10 PM): rapid turnover vehicles ready for next day
  - Weekend: different schedule (off-peak midweek is different)

- Infrastructure planning:
  - Deploy 80% charging capacity for overnight (vehicles parked 8 hours)
  - Deploy 20% fast chargers for midday top-ups
  - No charging infrastructure needed for nights (low demand)

**Competitive advantage:**
- Right-size charging infrastructure by actual off-peak patterns
- Avoid deploying chargers that sit idle 80% of the time
- Focus on high-utilization charging windows

**Financial impact:** ₦100M-₦200M in optimized charging infrastructure (avoid stranded assets)
